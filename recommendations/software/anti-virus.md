<h2>Just use Windows Defender.</h2>
<p class="av-banner-lede">Microsoft Defender Antivirus ships with every copy of Windows&nbsp;10 and 11. It scores 99.93% online protection in the latest AV&#8209;Comparatives round with only 3 false positives, and updates silently in the background. For the overwhelming majority of home users this is the right answer and the final answer.</p>
<ol class="av-banner-steps">
<li><span>1</span> Open Start, type <strong>Windows Security</strong>.</li>
<li><span>2</span> Confirm <strong>Virus &amp; threat protection</strong> is on.</li>
<li><span>3</span> Done. Close the tab.</li>
</ol>
<p class="av-banner-foot">Paying for a third&#8209;party AV will not make you safer. It will eat memory, throw pop&#8209;ups, and add an extra attacker surface. Only pay if one of the use cases in the next section actually fits.</p>
</div>

<h2>Picks, in detail</h2>
<p class="cpu-section-lede">One paragraph per tier &mdash; what you get, what to watch for, when to actually pull the trigger.</p>

<div class="cpu-details">

<article class="cpu-detail">
</aside>
<aside class="cpu-detail-aside">
<span class="cpu-detail-no">01</span>
<span class="cpu-detail-band">Microsoft Defender</span>
<div class="cpu-detail-chip">Bundled with Windows</div>
<div class="cpu-detail-price">&pound;0<small>included</small></div>
<dl class="cpu-detail-specs">
<div><dt>Engine</dt><dd>MS Defender</dd></div>
<div><dt>Protect</dt><dd>99.93%</dd></div>
<div><dt>Impact</dt><dd>12.9 (Adv)</dd></div>

</dl>
</aside>
<div class="cpu-detail-body">
<h3>Same engine, plus a dashboard that follows you onto Mac and mobile.</h3>
<p>Microsoft Defender is included with Windows. The consumer Defender app also offers cross-device apps for Mac, iPhone and Android; on Windows it&rsquo;s the same scanner you already had &mdash; the value is a central place to see device status. Scores 99.93% online protection with only 3 false alarms in the March 2026 round &mdash; tied with ESET on protection, beats it on false positives.</p>
<p>Install the Defender app from Microsoft if you want the cross-device dashboard. There is no reason to pay separately for Defender &mdash; the built-in scanner gives identical protection on Windows. The one weak spot is system impact: 12.9 in the April 2026 performance test, which puts it in the &ldquo;Advanced&rdquo; band rather than &ldquo;Advanced+&rdquo;. On modern hardware you won&rsquo;t notice; on an older laptop ESET or Kaspersky will feel snappier.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="cpu-detail">
<aside class="cpu-detail-aside">
<span class="cpu-detail-no">02</span>

</dl>
</aside>
<div class="cpu-detail-body">
<h3>Independent, Slovak, light footprint, clean ownership story.</h3>
<p>ESET has stayed an independent company through two decades of consolidation in this market &mdash; that&rsquo;s the headline reason it&rsquo;s here. On the raw numbers in the latest AV&#8209;Comparatives rounds it sits at 99.93% online protection (tied with Defender), 4.2 system&#8209;impact (Advanced+, second only to Kaspersky among paid products), and 16 false positives in the March run &mdash; the highest of the three picks here.</p>
<p>The Premium tier adds a password manager, file encryption, and online banking protection. The Essential tier (~&pound;30) is just the scanner. The licence renews at full price &mdash; buy a new licence each year from a reseller instead of auto&#8209;renewing.</p>
</div>
</article>

<span class="cpu-detail-no">03</span>
<span class="cpu-detail-band">Kaspersky Plus</span>
<div class="cpu-detail-chip">Kaspersky, Russia/UK</div>
<div class="cpu-detail-price">&pound;35<small>per year</small></div>
<dl class="cpu-detail-specs">
<div><dt>Engine</dt><dd>Kaspersky</dd></div>
<div><dt>Protect</dt><dd>99.97%</dd></div>

<div><dt>False&nbsp;+</dt><dd>2</dd></div>
</dl>

<div class="cpu-detail-body">
<h3>Best raw numbers on the page &mdash; with caveats you should weigh first.</h3>
<p>In the latest AV&#8209;Comparatives rounds Kaspersky is the strongest paid product on every axis that matters: 99.97% online protection, 3.5 system&#8209;impact (lightest of any paid AV here), and just <strong>two</strong> false positives in the March 2026 run &mdash; the lowest of any vendor tested. The Plus tier adds a VPN and password manager.</p>
<p>Caveats: the US government banned the sale of Kaspersky products in 2024 over national&#8209;security concerns, and the UK&rsquo;s NCSC advises against it for organisations doing work likely to be of interest to the Russian state. For a home user in the UK with no such exposure it&rsquo;s a legitimate technical choice and the engine is genuinely class&#8209;leading. If that context bothers you at all, pick ESET instead and pay a small protection&#8209;rate tax for the cleaner story.</p>
</div>
</article>

<section id="avoid" class="cpu-section">
<header class="cpu-section-h">
<span class="cpu-section-no">&sect; 05</span>
<h2>The Gen Digital tangle</h2>
<p class="cpu-section-lede">Four brands you&rsquo;ve heard of, one parent company, mostly one engine. Why none of them are on the list above.</p>
</header>

<div class="av-avoid">
<div class="av-avoid-icon"><span class="lg-i">{% include icons/warning.svg.html %}</span></div>
<div class="av-avoid-body">
<h3>Avast, AVG, Avira and Norton are all the same company now.</h3>
<p>NortonLifeLock bought Avast (which already owned AVG) in 2022 and renamed itself <strong>Gen Digital</strong>. Avira had been bought by NortonLifeLock the year before. So Avast, AVG, Avira and Norton are all the same parent company, and <strong>Avast, AVG and Norton now ship on the same shared scanning engine</strong>. Differences between them are mostly branding, bundles and pricing.</p>
<p>You can see it directly in the table above: Avast, AVG and Norton all return the same 99.97% protection rate and the same 9 false alarms in the March 2026 test, with system&#8209;impact scores within 0.2 points of each other. Three brands, one engine, three price points. Avira is still on a separate engine but is owned by the same group.</p>
<p>That consolidation is the reason none of them are on the recommendation list. Three of the four brands you remember as &ldquo;good&rdquo; from a decade ago are now one product wearing different colour schemes, sold through the same upsell funnel, with the same telemetry history &mdash; Avast was caught selling user browsing data through its Jumpshot subsidiary in 2020 and fined by the US FTC in 2024 over the same conduct. If you already have one installed and it&rsquo;s not nagging you, fine &mdash; you&rsquo;re not in danger. But on a clean install in 2026 there&rsquo;s no version of &ldquo;I want a paid AV&rdquo; where one of the Gen Digital brands beats either ESET or Kaspersky on the metrics that matter.</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<h2>How I choose</h2>
<p class="cpu-section-lede">The five factors that drive every pick on this page.</p>
</header>

<dl class="cpu-criteria">
<div>
<dt>Independent testing</dt>
<dd>Numbers on this page come from AV&#8209;Comparatives <strong>Real&#8209;World Protection (Feb&ndash;Mar&nbsp;2026)</strong>, <strong>Malware Protection Test (Mar&nbsp;2026)</strong> and <strong>Performance Test (Apr&nbsp;2026)</strong>. No vendor marketing.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>System impact</dt>
<dd>A scanner that slows your machine is one you will disable. Impact scores come from AV&#8209;Comparatives&rsquo; April 2026 performance test &mdash; file copy, install, launch, browsing. Lower is better.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>Ownership</dt>
<dd>One Microsoft, one independent EU vendor (ESET), one independent vendor with a known geopolitical caveat (Kaspersky). Anything inside Gen Digital is off the list.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>Price honesty</dt>
<dd>First&#8209;year price vs. renewal price. ESET and Kaspersky both auto&#8209;renew at full price &mdash; treat them as one&#8209;year purchases and rebuy each renewal.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>Default first</dt>
<dd>The built&#8209;in Windows scanner is the right answer for almost everyone. The bar for recommending a paid alternative is &ldquo;you have a specific reason&rdquo;, not &ldquo;it scored slightly higher in one round&rdquo;.</dd>
</div>
</dl>
