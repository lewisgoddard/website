<header class="rec-section-h">
<span class="rec-section-no">&sect; 02</span>
<h2>Picks, in detail</h2>
<p class="rec-section-lede">One paragraph per tier &mdash; what it&rsquo;s for, what to pair it with, what to walk away from.</p>
</header>

<div class="rec-details">

<article class="rec-detail">
<aside class="rec-detail-aside">
<span class="rec-detail-no">01</span>
<span class="rec-detail-band">Office &amp; HTPC</span>
<div class="rec-detail-chip">AMD Ryzen 5 8500G</div>
<div class="rec-detail-price">&pound;119<small>RRP</small></div>
<dl class="rec-detail-specs">
<div><dt>Socket</dt><dd>AM5</dd></div>
<div><dt>TDP</dt><dd>65 W</dd></div>
<div><dt>Verdict</dt><dd>Buy if you are not gaming.</dd></div>
</dl>
</aside>
<div class="rec-detail-body">
<h3>Six cores, integrated graphics, no fans on the GPU side.</h3>
<p>Boring, cheap, sips power, drives two 4K displays from the iGPU. For a family PC or a meeting-room box this is the right answer.</p>
<p>Pair with a B650 board, 32&nbsp;GB DDR5&#8209;5200, and a quiet case. Total system cost lands well under &pound;400. The Radeon 740M iGPU handles 1080p video playback and light gaming without drama. If you later add a GPU, the AM5 platform keeps pace with Zen&nbsp;5 IPC.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="rec-detail">
<aside class="rec-detail-aside">
<span class="rec-detail-no">02</span>
<span class="rec-detail-band">Budget Gaming</span>
<div class="rec-detail-chip">AMD Ryzen 5 9600X</div>
<div class="rec-detail-price">&pound;219<small>RRP</small></div>
<dl class="rec-detail-specs">
<div><dt>Socket</dt><dd>AM5</dd></div>
<div><dt>TDP</dt><dd>65 W</dd></div>
<div><dt>Verdict</dt><dd>Buy under &pound;300 builds.</dd></div>
</dl>
</aside>
<div class="rec-detail-body">
<h3>Cheapest chip that does not bottleneck a current-gen GPU.</h3>
<p>Zen 5 at the low end. Fast single-thread, sensible thermals, pairs happily with a B650 board and DDR5&#8209;6000. Pair with a 7800&nbsp;XT or 5070.</p>
<p>At 65&nbsp;W the 9600X runs cool enough for a 120mm tower air cooler. Six cores is fine for every game shipping in 2026; nothing at the &pound;300 GPU range is bottlenecked by it. The 9600 (non-X) saves &pound;20 if you find one.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="rec-detail">
<aside class="rec-detail-aside">
<span class="rec-detail-no">03</span>
<span class="rec-detail-band">Sweet Spot Gaming</span>
<div class="rec-detail-chip">AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D</div>
<div class="rec-detail-price">&pound;449<small>RRP</small></div>
<dl class="rec-detail-specs">
<div><dt>Socket</dt><dd>AM5</dd></div>
<div><dt>TDP</dt><dd>120 W</dd></div>
<div><dt>Verdict</dt><dd>Buy if gaming is the main job.</dd></div>
</dl>
</aside>
<div class="rec-detail-body">
<h3>The 3D V&#8209;Cache chip that nothing else catches at 1080p / 1440p.</h3>
<p>Genuinely the fastest gaming CPU on the market and will be for another year. Eight cores is fine &mdash; the cache is doing the work.</p>
<p>Wants a 240mm AIO; a good tower air cooler will also do. Pair with a B650E or X670E board and DDR5&#8209;6000&nbsp;CL30. The productivity delta vs. the 9700X is small &mdash; if you encode or render frequently, look at the 9900X instead. If gaming is 80&nbsp;% of your usage, this is the only chip worth considering above &pound;250.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="rec-detail">
<aside class="rec-detail-aside">
<span class="rec-detail-no">04</span>
<span class="rec-detail-band">All&#8209;Rounder</span>
<div class="rec-detail-chip">AMD Ryzen 9 9900X</div>
<div class="rec-detail-price">&pound;499<small>RRP</small></div>
<dl class="rec-detail-specs">
<div><dt>Socket</dt><dd>AM5</dd></div>
<div><dt>TDP</dt><dd>120 W</dd></div>
<div><dt>Verdict</dt><dd>Buy for mixed workstation / gaming.</dd></div>
</dl>
</aside>
<div class="rec-detail-body">
<h3>Twelve cores for the days you also compile, render or transcode.</h3>
<p>Strong at games, strong at threaded workloads, doesn&rsquo;t need a 360mm AIO. The chip I&rsquo;d put in my own desktop right now.</p>
<p>Twelve Zen&nbsp;5 cores at 5.6&nbsp;GHz boost. A 240mm AIO keeps it happy. This is the build for a developer who games: compile fast enough that the wait is never the bottleneck, and game at 1440p without apology. The 9950X only makes sense if you genuinely saturate 12 cores regularly.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="rec-detail">
<aside class="rec-detail-aside">
<span class="rec-detail-no">05</span>
<span class="rec-detail-band">Workstation / Top End</span>
<div class="rec-detail-chip">AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D</div>
<div class="rec-detail-price">&pound;729<small>RRP</small></div>
<dl class="rec-detail-specs">
<div><dt>Socket</dt><dd>AM5</dd></div>
<div><dt>TDP</dt><dd>170 W</dd></div>
<div><dt>Verdict</dt><dd>Buy if money is not the constraint.</dd></div>
</dl>
</aside>
<div class="rec-detail-body">
<h3>Sixteen cores, X3D cache, the no&#8209;compromises pick.</h3>
<p>If you need both render performance and X3D gaming speed in one socket, this is what you buy. Otherwise the 9900X above is the better value.</p>
<p>170&nbsp;W under full load; needs a quality 360mm AIO and a board with solid VRMs. An X670E is the right pairing. The 9950X3D beats the non-X3D in both gaming and the majority of production workloads. If budget is a constraint at all, buy the 9900X and spend the difference on more storage or a better GPU.</p>
</div>
</article>

<section id="criteria" class="rec-section">
<header class="rec-section-h">
<span class="rec-section-no">&sect; 05</span>
<h2>How I choose</h2>
<p class="rec-section-lede">The five factors that drive every pick on this page.</p>
</header>

<dl class="rec-criteria">
<div>
<dt>Socket longevity</dt>
<dd>AM5 has another generation of CPUs committed. LGA1851 may or may not. Lean AM5 unless you specifically need Intel acceleration.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>Memory</dt>
<dd>DDR5&#8209;6000 CL30 is the platform sweet spot on AMD. Faster kits don&rsquo;t help; slower kits cost you frame time.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>Cooling</dt>
<dd>A 65&nbsp;W chip is happy on a tower air cooler. 120&nbsp;W X3D parts want a 240mm AIO. 170&nbsp;W X3D wants 360mm.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>Platform price</dt>
<dd>Don&rsquo;t pair a &pound;700 CPU with a &pound;120 board. Match VRMs to the part you bought.</dd>
</div>
<div>
<dt>Honest scoring</dt>
<dd>Gaming and multi&#8209;thread numbers above are normalised to the 9800X3D and 9950X3D respectively. &ldquo;100&rdquo; means class&#8209;leader; not a real percentage.</dd>
</div>
</dl>
